How it works

Six steps. The first two cost nothing.

1
A conversation You, the player and Deng. What the family wants, and what the player wants. They're not always the same thing and it's better to find that out now.
2
An honest evaluation Where this player really stands, from coaches who see them every week. Measured numbers, not opinions. If the answer's not yet, you hear it here.
3
A plan with the real cost on it Tuition, flights, insurance, all of it, before anyone commits to anything. No family should find out what this costs in month three.
4
Profile and tape Measured profile sheet, game footage and a cut highlight reel. What a coach abroad asks for before they ask anything else.
5
Matching and introductions We go to programmes we know, or ones our contacts in that market know. Fit first. The biggest name isn't the right name for most players.
6
Admissions, visa and landing Paperwork, school admissions, the visa, and staying in touch once they're out there. The first three months abroad are the hard bit.

The small print, up front

Things we won't do.

  • We won't tell you your kid is ready when they're not. The honest no is the most useful thing on this page.
  • We won't charge you to be looked at. The conversation and the evaluation are free. Paying to be seen is the oldest trick in this business.
  • We won't promise a scholarship. Nobody honest can. Anyone who does is selling you something.
  • We won't put a percentage on it. We'll put names on it instead, and you can go and check them.
  • We won't send a young player abroad because the family can afford it. It has to be right for the player, at the right age, or it's just an expensive way to fall out of love with the game.
Questions we get

What parents actually ask.

  • How does a Danish player end up in an American high school? Almost always through prep or high school first, then college. You need tape, measured numbers, school grades and someone on the other end who knows the programme. That's the whole job.
  • How old should they be? Fifteen or sixteen is the usual window for high school and prep. Younger than that and we'll normally tell you to wait, and we'll tell you why.
  • What does it cost? Depends entirely on the school and how much aid comes with it. You get the full number, including flights and insurance, before anyone signs anything. Our first conversation and evaluation are free.
  • Will they get a scholarship? Maybe. We won't promise it and neither should anybody else.
  • What if they're not good enough yet? Then we say so and we tell you what to work on. Most players who ask us aren't ready in the first year. That isn't a no forever.
  • Can they come home? Yes, and some do. Malthe went to Canada for a year and came back. That year still counts.
  • Do they need to be a Talata player? No. If you're at another club in Denmark and you want an honest read, get in touch.
