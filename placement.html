Most placement services won't name a single player.

You'll see a ninety nine percent success rate on a website that can't tell you one kid it actually sent anywhere. We're doing it the other way round.

Every player we help goes on this page by name, with the school and the year.

Malthe joined our Academy for the year and we ran the process with him. He went to Orangeville Prep in Ontario. Johan came up through the club from the very first season, and we'll be straight about that one: we built the player, his family ran the move.

Here is exactly who does what. We do the evaluation, the film, the profile and the relationship, because we are the ones who see the player every week. The placement itself runs through licensed partner agencies who handle school matching, admissions and the visa. We stay in it the whole way and we stay on the phone once they land.

Where we place

Four routes out. We'll tell you which one is yours.

Most families turn up asking about one of these. Usually it isn't the right one yet. First job is working out which road the player's actually on.

High school and prep, USA and Canada

The most common way into American college basketball, and the road Johan's on. School matching, admissions, host family or dorm, and the visa paperwork that comes with it.

USA · Canada

Clubs and academies in Europe

Sometimes the right step up is a short flight, not another continent. Spanish and other European academies take young players seriously and the basketball education is excellent, and a fifteen year old stays inside the same time zone as home.

College basketball in the USA

NCAA Division 1 and 2, NAIA and JUCO. Eligibility, academics and tape, sorted early, because the academic side ends more of these than the basketball side does.

How it works

Six steps. The first two cost nothing.

1
A conversation You, the player and Deng. What the family wants, and what the player wants. They're not always the same thing and it's better to find that out now.
2
An honest evaluation Where this player really stands, from coaches who see them every week. Measured numbers, not opinions. If the answer's not yet, you hear it here.
3
A plan with the real cost on it Tuition, flights, insurance, all of it, before anyone commits to anything. No family should find out what this costs in month three.
4
Profile and full game film Measured profile sheet, a cut highlight reel, and full games, uncut. The highlight reel gets you opened. The full film is what gets you an answer, because that's where a coach sees what a player does when the ball isn't theirs.
5
Matching and introductions We go to programmes we know, or ones our contacts in that market know. Fit first. The biggest name isn't the right name for most players.
6
Admissions, visa and landing Handled by our partner agency, with us in every conversation. Then we stay in touch once they're out there, because the first three months abroad are the hard bit.

Partner agencies

The placement process runs through established agencies we work with, including FIBA licensed representation. The club doesn't act as the agent and doesn't pretend to.

Coming in 2027: NIL. American college athletes can earn from their name, image and likeness now, and the rules for international athletes are moving fast. We're building that into the advice instead of pretending it isn't there.

The small print, up front

Things we won't do.

  • We won't tell you your kid is ready when they're not. The honest no is the most useful thing on this page.
  • We won't charge you to be looked at. The conversation and the evaluation are free. Paying to be seen is the oldest trick in this business.
  • We won't promise a scholarship. Nobody honest can. Anyone who does is selling you something.
  • We won't put a percentage on it. We'll put names on it instead, and you can go and check them.
  • We won't send a young player abroad because the family can afford it. It has to be right for the player, at the right age, or it's just an expensive way to fall out of love with the game.
Questions we get

What parents actually ask.

  • How does a Danish player end up in an American high school? Almost always through prep or high school first, then college. You need tape, measured numbers, school grades and someone on the other end who knows the programme. That's the whole job.
  • How old should they be? Fifteen or sixteen is the usual window for high school and prep. Younger than that and we'll normally tell you to wait, and we'll tell you why.
  • What does it cost? Depends entirely on the school and how much aid comes with it. You get the full number, including flights and insurance, before anyone signs anything. Our first conversation and evaluation are free.
  • Will they get a scholarship? Maybe. We won't promise it and neither should anybody else.
  • What if they're not good enough yet? Then we say so and we tell you what to work on. Most players who ask us aren't ready in the first year. That isn't a no forever.
  • Can they come home? Yes, and some do. Malthe went to Canada for a year and came back. That year still counts.
  • Do they need to be a Talata player? No. If you're at another club in Denmark and you want an honest read, get in touch.
