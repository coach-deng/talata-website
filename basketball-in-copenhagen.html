A guide from Talata Basketball

Basketball in Copenhagen

Where to play, which group you belong in, what it costs, and how to try it before you commit to anything.

The short version. Basketball in Copenhagen is run by clubs, not by drop-in classes, and you pay per half-season rather than per session. Almost every club will let you train once for free before you decide. If you are on Østerbro or can get there, Talata trains at Svanemøllehallen and Strandvejsskolen with groups from age 5 to adult, and your first session is free.

Find the group you belong in

Danish clubs sort by age, and the age band matters more than how good you are. A complete beginner and an experienced player of the same age train together. The coaching adjusts, the group doesn't.

What it actually costs

The half-season fee is what surprises people coming from a pay-per-class country. It's not a monthly subscription and it's not per session. It covers every session that group runs for half a year. For the Academy that is four sessions a week, so the per-session cost lands well under what a single private class costs anywhere in the city.

Before you budget, four things:

  • The first session is free, at Talata and at most Copenhagen clubs. Nobody expects you to pay before you've seen a session.
  • You do not need gear. Indoor trainers and a water bottle. We bring the balls. Club gear is optional and comes later.
  • Sibling discounts exist at most clubs, ours included. Ask, don't assume.
  • Financial support exists. If money is the only thing stopping a kid playing, say so. Danish clubs have routes for this, and we'd rather have the player.

Where the clubs are

Copenhagen has a real basketball scene and it's spread right across the city. We're on Østerbro, so we're the easy answer if you live in 2100 or can get there after school. Anywhere else, geography beats everything. A brilliant club 45 minutes away loses to a decent one down the road, because the kid has to want to go twice a week in January.

Other clubs run programmes in Amager, Nordvest, Nørrebro and the inner city, among them Stevnsgade, BK Amager, Falkon and Brøernes Basketball Klub. We work with Stevnsgade directly. Our coaches run their older girls' teams. So if a girls' pathway near Nørrebro suits you better, tell us and we'll point you there.

If you want to just shoot around with no club attached, the outdoor courts at Fælledparken and the courts around Nørrebro run informal pickup through the warmer months, and we open our own gym for drop-in sessions during the season.

How a first session actually goes

Turn up ten minutes early, we'll find you, and you train with your age group. No assessment, no trial period to survive. Younger groups spend the hour playing games that happen to build coordination and ball handling. Older groups train the way they play. Small-sided games with constraints, not lines of kids waiting their turn at a cone.

Nobody gets put on the spot on day one. Most kids who join us have never touched a basketball. The group is used to it, because most of them arrived the same way.

Common questions

Can my child start mid-season?

Yes. We take new players all year and the fee is prorated if you join late. The season runs August to December, then January to June. Nobody gets turned away in October.

Does my child need to speak Danish?

No. We coach in English and Danish. About a third of our players come from international families, and that's part of why the club looks the way it does.

What if they try it and hate it?

Then they've lost an hour and you've lost nothing. That's what the free session is for. Better a kid works out in one hour that basketball isn't their sport, than you pay 1,800 kr to find out in November.

Is there a girls-only option?

Yes, Mondays. Talata Sparks is girls-only, ages 6 to 14. Our older girls help coach it, which is the bit the little ones actually turn up for. Girls are welcome in every mixed group too.

Try a session. It costs nothing.
